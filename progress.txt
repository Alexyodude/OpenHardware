  KEY FINDING: the 8088 CLEARS AF on AND/OR/XOR. The manual calls it
  "undefined". Leaving it carried cost exactly 50% of every logical opcode --
  it passed only where AF happened to be clear already. Verified across
  60,000 cases: AF set beforehand ~50%, set afterwards 0.

=== 2026-08-24 US-001: RESETS with atomic aliases ===
Landed two GENERIC mechanisms in core/cortexm0 (no chip addresses):
  map_alias(addr, target, op)  -- write applies store/set/clear/xor to target
  map_mirror(addr, source, invert, mask) -- read derives from another register
RP2040 specifics live in webui/boards/rp2040.py, adopted from the (unadopted)
docs/research/peripheral-layer/rp2040_v2/ with its datasheet citations.

LEARNINGS:
- Aliases are per-REGISTER address arithmetic, not per-peripheral: the SET view
  of base+0x08 is base+0x2008. Aliasing the peripheral stacks every register's
  alias on the first one.
- SIO is the chip's ONLY peripheral without bus-level aliases (datasheet 2.1.2).
  Its GPIO registers carry dedicated SET/CLR/XOR registers instead. The research
  had corrected itself here already: the rule is per-peripheral, NOT "APB range"
  -- SSI at 0x18000000 is far from APB and does have aliases.
- RESET_DONE must be a MIRROR, not storage. Drivers spin on it; stored, the
  loop never terminates -- a hang that looks like a bad instruction.
- Session-scoped pytest fixture + new mutable state = cross-test leakage.
  clear_memory() deliberately does NOT clear the register map (contents vs
  configuration), so an autouse fixture calls clear_registers().
- Mutation testing found `read_only` was DEAD: a mirror's write lands in its own
  word and its read comes from `source`, so ignoring the write changed nothing
  observable. Removed rather than kept as a flag that looks load-bearing.
