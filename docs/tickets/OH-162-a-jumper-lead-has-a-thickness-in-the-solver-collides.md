A jumper lead here is a chain of points. It falls, sags, springs and stays out of the chips -- and it has no thickness in the solver, passes straight through other wires, and settles with half its tube inside whatever it rests on. OH-132 just made that last fact measurable: every settled wire reports `proud` = the full radius, 0.16 units, because collide() pushes the node's CENTRE to the face and the tube is drawn around it afterwards.

What is modelled, all in webui/static/wires.js, all with its constants labelled as the judgements they are (GRAVITY -36 "not 9.81", DAMPING 0.982 standing in for air and stiffness, STIFFNESS 0.4 as a two-node bend constraint, SLACK 1.35, NODES 22, ITERATIONS 14, SUBSTEP 1/120): a Verlet rope pinned at both ends, colliding with the parts' boxes and spheres, the board slab and a floor, contact run last in each pass.

What is not, measured 2026-09-04:

1. The tube's radius is not in the solver. collide() and outOfBox() move points; WIRE_RADIUS (0.16, board3d.js) is a drawing parameter. A lead lying on a chip is drawn half-buried; the OH-132 probe reads proud = 0.16 on every resting node.
2. Wires do not collide with wires. wires.js has no reference to any other Rope; two leads laid across each other occupy the same space. There is no test asserting either way.
3. Bend stiffness is a two-node constraint and nothing holds a bend once made -- a real lead keeps the shape it was pushed into (plastic set), which is what makes a breadboard tidy; this one relaxes to the catenary every time.
4. No oracle. Every number is a stated judgement, which is allowed here (BOARD_MASK's rule), but nothing has been compared to a real lead: no photograph, no measured sag.

Comparison, read 2026-09-04: Xplora3D draws wires on a breadboard with "Auto Snap Pins" and exports GLB; its wire physics, if any, is inside an engine bundle that loads after login and was not inspected. Simulator86 has no 3D and no physics -- a 2D diagram with pins. Neither is an oracle either; a photograph of a real lead on a real XIAO is, and no such photograph exists in this tree.

STEPS, in the order that keeps each falsifiable:

1. Radius in the solver: collide() pushes a node to face + WIRE_RADIUS (and sphere r + radius), with the radius passed in from the caller so wires.js still knows nothing about three.js. The OH-132 contact() probe is the check: after this, a resting wire's `proud` reads 0 and `inside` stays 0. Expect the drape to change visibly on every board; screenshot before and after.
2. Wire-wire contact: capsule-capsule between node segments of different ropes, run in the same last-pass position as the solids, with the same radius. Test: two leads crossed at right angles settle with a centre gap of at least 2 x radius at the crossing.
3. Plastic set, if it earns its place: record the rest angle at each node when a wire stops moving and relax toward it, so a bent lead stays bent. Judgement, labelled; the test is that a lead dragged into an arc does not return to a catenary.
4. The oracle: one photograph of one lead of known length on one board, and the sag it shows against the sag this model gives for the same span. That single number is what would retire GRAVITY, DAMPING and SLACK as judgements, and it costs a phone.

Each step is a separate commit with its own mutation-tested assertion (OH-132's lesson: every fixture the author used TOUCHED the box, and the reviewer's clear-air fixture is what found the bug -- include one fixture that should report nothing).

**collide() runs twice, and the review is why.** Growth is per solid and resolution is sequential, so a node ejected onto solid B's grown face lands inside solid A's real box -- and a chip sitting flush on a board is the everyday case, its grown bottom face a radius BELOW the slab's top. Measured on a wire pinned to two pads over a slab with one chip: contact().inside went 0 before OH-162 and 0.16 with the first version of this change, which is the number this file itself calls the fault. So a radius is a preference and being outside a solid is a requirement: the grown solids are resolved, then the UNGROWN ones are resolved again, which is the guarantee the file had before OH-162 and restores that same 0. It restores the old invariant rather than improving on it -- two genuinely overlapping ungrown solids would still trap a node, exactly as before, and the reviewer measured 13 such pairs on the Uno alone (a through-hole body passing through the PCB) with no wire able to reach any of them.

**And the guard on that second pass had a fixed point, which was the review's second catch.** Skipping when the node had not MOVED is not the same as skipping when nothing PUSHED: two solids 0.05 apart push a node out and straight back inside one pass, so its coordinates come out identical while both pushes really happened, and the node stays 0.11 inside the first box for ever because it is a fixed point of the cycle. outOfSolids now returns whether a push executed and collide gates on that; outOfBox and outOfSphere return booleans. Verified on the reviewer's geometry: starting at the fixed point -0.135 now ends at -0.025, the same as starting anywhere else. Counted through the real collide: the second pass ran 133,885 times against the first pass's 336,000, 39.9%.

**Nothing in this product bends a lead that is connected at both ends, and that is the shortest reason.** Step 3's own acceptance test -- "a lead dragged into an arc does not return to a catenary" -- describes an interaction the interface does not have. No GESTURE moves a finished lead's ends: `addPart` sets a part's position once from the bench slot its index gives, `removePart` splices the list without re-laying out the rest, no pointer handler reaches a part at all, and a wire touching a removed part is discarded rather than left to swing. `moveHead` and `moveTail` are called every frame for every wire and hand over the same point every frame -- worst end movement over six hundred frames of the animation loop's own calls is exactly 0, so a gate on "an end moved" would in fact be a perfect signal and would simply never fire.

**A first draft of this note said no end ever moves, full stop, and a second review broke it.** Two load-time paths move one. `placeModel` raises every pad to the real board's top face, which on the Uno is 0.68 to 5.12 scene units, so the head of a wire made while the vendor model was still in flight jumps **4.44 units** -- six times what a part lifts a lead in the fixture below. `carvePins` then swaps that wire's `post` to the carved pin, which sits at the model's own millimetre position rather than at layout pixels. Both fire once per board load, for a wire made during the fetch, which is OH-129's case and has its own test in `test_board3d.py`.

**That is where a bend memory has its best case, and the sweep had excluded it.** The measurement is the second review's own, reproduced here by running its probe rather than taken on its word. Retention on the pad raise: 0.023 units at rate 1e-4, 0.126 at 1e-3, 0.168 at 3e-3, 0.209 at 0.01 -- 1.31 wire radii, and twenty times the 0.0106 this decline otherwise rests on. It does not change the answer, and the reason it does not is worth writing down rather than glossing: a person cannot produce that shape on purpose, it happens once per load and never again, and what would be remembered is the bend a pad had **before it moved to where it belongs**. A shape nobody asked for, kept from a position that no longer exists, is a defect and not the feature step 3 wanted.

**One path does move an end**, and the review found it: `carvePins` swaps a wire's `post` to the carved pin when a vendor model lands (board3d.js:1419-1433), and the pin sits at the model's own millimetre position while the placeholder sat at layout pixels, so `anchorOf` answers a different point the frame after. It fires once per board load, for a wire made while the model was still in flight, which is OH-129's case. Not a gesture and not repeatable by a person.

So the one manipulation left is a collider arriving under a lead or leaving from under it, because `refreshColliders` runs on every add and every remove -- and that is the manoeuvre this step has to be measured on.

It is the gap between a straight rest and no constraint at all, which is the straightening direction only; a remembered rest SHORTER than the current bend pushes the other way, which the shipped constraint never does, and nothing measures that bound. What is measured is the outcome.

**The ticket's own trigger is unsafe on top of all that.** Its words are "record the rest angle when a wire stops moving". The largest bend a lead ever sees is its own creation, thirty-four times anything a hand can hold. Any threshold that records a hand records the birth, and recording the birth changes the drape of every lead on every board: measured at yield 0.02, resting sag 4.04 becomes 1.49.

**Three of my own measurements were wrong first and the third nearly settled it the wrong way.** A first probe read 0.153 units of "shape memory" that was entirely `payOut`: pulling a lead's ends further apart lengthens it for good, so the shape it came back to was a longer lead's shape. A second manoeuvre swung the tail a quarter turn about the head at constant distance, which only ROTATES the lead -- a bend ratio is rotation-invariant, so it measured nothing and read 9.3e-6. And the whole first case was built on a fold, the tail walked in to 30% of the span and back, which is not reachable at all.

**The fixture trap, which is the one worth remembering.** On the reachable manoeuvre I first read 0.85 units kept, 5.3 wire radii, and it looked like the decision flipping. It was the memory recording a torn wire. `outOfBox` sends a node out by its NEAREST face, so two neighbours inside a solid narrower than the lead dips into it leave by opposite faces and are held apart at the solid's width: with the memory OFF and the shipped solver, a box three units across under a lead dipping two and a half into it reported **417% over rest**, and one 1.6 across reported 199%. The memory then recorded that and enforced it: unclamped it reached bend ratios of 3.1626, which two segments cannot span, and left the lead 68% over rest with nothing touching it. A part eight units across and two and a half tall is one a lead drapes over -- five nodes rest on its top face and the worst stretch is 0.55% -- and on that fixture the kept shape is the 0.0106 above.

**A memory of a length has to be clamped to what the geometry allows.** Clamping the recorded ratio at 1 takes the same jammed fixture from 68% over rest to 2.3%. Anyone implementing this later needs that line before anything else.

**That tearing is a defect and it is now OH-165**, filed 2026-09-05, along with the larger finding it led to: a solid narrower than the gap between two nodes falls BETWEEN them and the lead threads through it, with `inside`, `proud` and `worstStretch` all reading clean. On a real Uno header at real scale a 28-unit lead's segment passes through the full half-width while stretch reads 1.46%. A first draft of this note called that "a few per cent and invisible"; it was measuring the wrong quantity, in exactly the way OH-132's own probe did, and the review caught it. OH-165 has the table.

**Two gaps recorded rather than excused.** Nothing in `tests/webui/test_wires.py` checks what the bending constraint is FOR. Stub `stiffen` with an early return and 23 of its 25 tests pass, including `test_a_stiff_wire_hangs_less_deeply_than_a_limp_one`, which is the one named for it -- few 2.85 against many 3.24, energy still falling and stretch still under one per cent. Two do fail, and how they fail is the point: the wire-wire pair reaches 0.31995 where two tubes need 0.32, short by sixteen parts in a hundred thousand, because a bendless lead drapes a little differently. An accident of the drape, not a check on bending. **A first draft of this note said nothing kills it at all**, which was a claim about the whole file taken from a review that had run one test -- the same mistake as the bound, caught the same way. That test's docstring also gives a reason measurement refutes -- it says a 4-node wire sags less because "there is nothing for it to bend between", but deleting the constraint WIDENS that gap from 0.2045 to 0.3930, and at 4 nodes the constraint increases sag rather than reducing it. The inequality holds because a 3-segment chain of the same total length sags less than a 21-segment one, which is arithmetic and not stiffness. And `test_a_settled_wire_keeps_its_own_length` asserts under 1% with no collider in its fixture, while a lead over a real header reads 2.0%.

**What was NOT measured, and it is the honest gap in the decline.** A directional memory. The distance from a node to the one two along is degenerate about the bend axis: it can hold a lead bunched but never bent in a chosen plane, and gravity picks the plane. Everything above says this scalar formulation does not earn its place; none of it says no formulation does.

**What would change the answer** is a lead whose bending mattered -- `NODES`, `STIFFNESS`, or a pair spanning further than two segments -- and every one of those changes the drape of every lead on every board. It also needs a gesture, because there is nothing here that bends a connected lead. Step 4's photograph is the wrong oracle for this: it is about sag and retires `GRAVITY`, `DAMPING` and `SLACK`. Reopening step 3 wants a different photograph, of a lead bent by hand that stayed bent, and a way to bend one here.

**A separate bug found on the same read of board3d.js**, recorded here because it has no other home yet: `addPart` positions from `row = view.parts.length` and `removePart` splices without renumbering, so removing part 0 of three and adding a fourth places it at row 2, on top of the part already there. No wire's end moves, so the claim above holds, but it is the sharpest reachable way to put a solid under a settled lead.

It is asserted at 1e-4 rather than at what was measured, because pinning float noise as a requirement makes a test fail on any harmless change; 1e-4 is 1,490 times the noise and still kills every rate that does anything. It also asserts the lead is under 1% over rest and settled below 1e-9 of energy afterwards -- both were collected and printed without being asserted in a first draft, which is the exact pattern a step-2 review already caught on this ticket.

**Its second arm is what makes the first a measurement.** A lead does carry one memory -- its own length -- and `payOut` only ever grows, so a tail dragged out to 16 and brought back to 10 leaves the rest length 21% longer and the settled shape **1.75 units** from where it hung. Seven orders of magnitude above the drape's. A test that cannot see a memory that is there says nothing about one that is not. Its controls are that the part must lift the lead by half a unit, at least three nodes must come to rest on its top face, worst stretch under 2% so the fixture is not the torn one, nothing paid out, and a part arriving twenty units to the side leaving the lead bit-identical to one that never saw it.

**The test was proved able to fail rather than assumed to be.** The mechanism was patched into a copy of `wires.js` and this test's own body run against it, with every assertion evaluated at each rate. `returnedTo`: 1e-5 gives 8.3e-4, 1e-4 gives 6.2e-3, 3e-4 gives 1.0e-2, 1e-3 gives 1.1e-2, 3e-3 gives 2.6e-3, 0.01 gives 3.5e-4, and 0.1 and 1 give 8.2e-9 and 2.4e-9. At a bound of 1e-4 the first six die. **At the 1e-3 a first draft shipped, only four do.**
